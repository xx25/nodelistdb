How this is measured

Every node advertising the PING flag receives a netmail addressed to user PING from 2:5001/100 about once every two weeks, sent through the ordinary netmail routing (not a direct dial). FTS-4010 says the destination bounces it back quoting the ^AVia lines it collected on the way — that quoted chain is the outbound path shown here, and the answer's own Via chain is the return path. A node advertising TRACE should notify the sender when a PING merely passes through it: confirmed means at least one of our pings crossed it and it did, silent means pings crossed it and it never did, unobserved means no ping of ours has been routed through it in the last {{.Days}} days, so nothing is known. Unanswered pings are given 7 days; a late answer still counts.

Two limits of the TRACE column: only nodes that sit on 2:5001/100's own routes (its uplink, the zone and region hubs and the net hosts on the way) can ever be crossed, so most TRACE nodes stay unobserved by construction rather than by fault; and the verdict pairs paths from the last {{.Days}} days with the flags on the current nodelist, so a node that added or dropped TRACE inside that window is judged by its present flag.
